If it can't wait

Most of what happens online can be handled calmly, over a few days. These situations can't wait.

911 Someone is in danger right now

A threat of violence that could happen soon, someone who has been hurt, or a child who may act on thoughts of suicide.

988 Thoughts of suicide or self-harm

Stay with your child and call or text 988, or text HOME to 741741. If you're worried and not sure what to do, you can call 988 on your own.

NCMEC Someone wants, or threatens to
share, a sexual image

Don't pay and stop replying. Save the messages, never the image. Report it at report.cybertip.org or 1-800-843-5678.

Whatever happened, it's not your child's fault. The guide covers each of these in full.

Start here

Three things you can do today.

You don't need to read the whole guide to begin. These come straight from it.

Start from afar.

Many kids won't answer “Are you being bullied?” But many find it easier to talk about what's happening to other kids. Start there, and move closer only as far as they let you.

Take the fear out of telling.

Some kids stay quiet because they're afraid of losing their phone or getting in trouble. Tell them, before they even ask, that telling you will never get them punished.

Save it before you block it.

Blocking and reporting can make posts disappear. Take screenshots that show the message, the username, and the date first. For a sexual image of anyone under 18, save the messages, never the image.

If your child opens up, start with: “Thank you for telling me. It’s not your fault. We’ll figure this out together.”

Who it's from

From Kirk Smalley and Stand for the Silent.

Stand for the Silent was started in 2010 by a group of students in Oklahoma City, after they heard what happened to Kirk and Laura Smalley's son, Ty. Since then, Kirk has carried Ty's story into schools across the country. Many of the students who hear it write to him afterward, and those letters are the reason this guide exists.
