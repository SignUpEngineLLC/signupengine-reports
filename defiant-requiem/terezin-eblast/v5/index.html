November 24, 1941  |  85 years

Walk the streets of Terezín

Prisoners rehearse Verdi's Requiem in Terezín, June 23, 1944. Photograph by Maurice Rossel, the Red Cross delegate who toured the ghetto that day. ICRC archives, V-P-HIST-01161-33.

On November 24, 1941, 342 young Jewish men from Prague arrived at a garrison town north of the city. They had been sent to turn its barracks into a ghetto. Over the next three and a half years, about 140,000 people would pass through Terezín. About 34,000 died there, and about 88,000 were deported on to the camps and killing sites of the East.

Eighty-five years later, we invite you to walk its streets.

We have rebuilt our Terezín Ghetto Virtual Tour from the ground up. Choose any location on the map to see the building as it stands today, alongside photographs and drawings from the ghetto years, and to read what happened there.

Then and now

The town square, 1944 and today

June 23, 1944. An orchestra plays in the music pavilion built for the Red Cross visit. ICRC archives.

Today. The same square. A year before the concert it was fenced off under a circus tent, where prisoners packed crates for the German army.
 

New on the tour

Follow the Requiem

A path through the places where Rafael Schächter and his chorus of fellow prisoners learned Verdi's Requiem from a single score and sang it, as survivor Edgar Krasa recalled, to say to their captors what they could not say aloud. It begins in the barracks where the first transport arrived and ends at the rail siding.
